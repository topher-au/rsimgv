title: Playing video desc: Video playback in rsimgv with libmpv or GStreamer: controls, the seek overlay, speed, volume, remembered positions and mpv options. ---

Videos open in the same viewer as pictures. Playback is by libmpv unless you choose GTK's own GStreamer backend, and everything you do to a video is an action you can bind to a key.

{{shot video-viewer "A video playing, with the seek overlay at the top and the controls at the bottom"}}

Controls

Move the pointer toward the bottom of the viewer (the lowest fifth of it) and the video's controls appear: previous, play or pause, stop, next, a position slider with the elapsed and remaining time, fullscreen, auto-advance, and a mute button with a volume slider. P pins the controls so they stay shown.

Bindings that take a value

Four actions have no default key because each binding supplies its own amount. In the keybinding editor, choose the action, press a key, and set the value:

ActionValueExample
video.seekSeconds, −3600 to 3600; negative goes back.Shift+→ seeks +30 s.
video.volumePercent change, −100 to 100.+10% on Shift+↑.
video.set-speedA multiple of normal speed, 0.05 to 16.1 sets 1×, 2 sets 2×.

The same speed is reported by view.info in the remote control interface, along with the position, duration and frame rate.

The seek overlay

When you skip through a video, a progress bar and the time appear over it. It shows where you will land, so you can tap the key repeatedly and watch it move. The Overlay tab of the video preferences sets it up:

Remembered position and speed

rsimgv remembers where you left a video and the speed you were playing it at, and resumes from there next time. If you left it within two seconds of the start or the end, it starts from the beginning instead. The memory is keyed by the file's contents rather than its name, so it survives moving or renaming; see per-file settings.

Previews in the browse grid

In the browse grid a video's thumbnail is a frame a tenth of the way in, and resting the pointer on it plays a strip of frames spread through the video, made with ffmpeg. Video frame previews in Browsing → Thumbnails turns that off.

Choosing the backend

Player backend on the Player tab of the video preferences chooses what plays videos. The change applies to the next video you open.

{{shot prefs-videos "The Videos page of the preferences"}}
mpv (default)
libmpv, rendered in software into memory for GTK to show. SVP and mpv options apply to this backend.
gtk
GStreamer, through GTK's own media backend.

Configuring mpv

rsimgv starts mpv with sensible defaults: no window of its own, hardware decoding set to auto-safe, keep-open so a finished video stays on its last frame, no on-screen controller, and mpv's default key bindings off, because rsimgv handles the keys. Two places let you change that:

  1. mpv options in Videos → Player take mpv command-line options such as --hwdec=auto-copy. Options that would give mpv a window of its own (vo, wid, gpu-context, fs, fullscreen) are ignored.
  2. mpv.conf in ~/.config/rsimgv/mpv/ is an ordinary mpv config file kept with rsimgv's settings, along with mpv's scripts. rsimgv watches it, and players made after a change use the new options.

Driving mpv directly

Each player listens for mpv's JSON IPC on /tmp/rsimgv-mpv-<pid in hex> unless your options name another socket, so tools that speak mpv's protocol can reach it. The socket appears only once a video has been opened.

Rotated videos

libmpv's software renderer cannot rotate frames, and a quarter turn makes it fail. rsimgv leaves the renderer's rotation off and turns such videos upright with a filter instead, so a phone video shot in portrait plays the right way up.
