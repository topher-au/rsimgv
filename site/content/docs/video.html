title: Playing video desc: Video playback in rsimgv with libmpv or GStreamer: controls, seeking, speed, volume, crop and aspect ratio, the backdrop, remembered positions and mpv options. ---

Videos open in the same viewer as pictures. Playback is by libmpv unless you choose GTK's own GStreamer backend, and everything you do to a video is an action you can bind to a key.

Controls

Move the pointer toward the bottom of the viewer and the video's controls appear: previous, play or pause, stop, next, a position slider with the elapsed and remaining time, fullscreen, the slideshow, and a mute button with a volume slider. P pins the controls so they stay shown.

The controls are locked in place by default. Turn off Videos → Playback → Lock the video controls (video.controls.locked) and they can be dragged to a new place over the video, and their left or right edge dragged to change their width. The place and width are remembered.

Bindings that take a value

Several actions have no default key because each binding supplies its own amount. In the keybinding editor, choose the action, press a key, and set the value:

ActionValueExample
video.seekSeconds, −3600 to 3600; negative goes back.Shift+→ seeks +30 s.
video.frame-seekFrames, −1000 to 1000; negative goes back.One frame on Shift+→.
video.volumePercent change, −100 to 100.+10% on Shift+↑.
video.set-speedA multiple of normal speed, 0.05 to 16.1 sets 1×, 2 sets 2×.
video.adjust-speedPercent change, e.g. +25 for 0.25× faster.] speeds up, [ slows down.

The volume actions also take a target: Current Video (the default) changes the video shown or the next one, and Application changes rsimgv's own volume, which videos without a stored volume start at. The application volume is also video.app-volume, from 0 to 2. Mute has no target and always acts on the video shown. The volume display and button dim for a video with no sound, and the part above 100% is drawn darker.

The same speed is reported by view.info in the remote control interface, along with the position, duration and frame rate.

Starting and timing

  • Start delay (video.start-delay) waits this many milliseconds before starting a video reached with next or previous, so a quick step past it does not start it. 0 starts at once.
  • Scale times by playback speed (video.scaled-times) shows the elapsed and remaining time as the time they take at the current speed, so a ten-minute video at 25% speed shows forty minutes.

The seek display

When you skip through a video, a progress bar and the time appear over it. It shows where you will land, so you can tap the key repeatedly and watch it move. This is one element of the configurable media overlay; the Overlay page under Viewer places it, chooses whether the bar and text show, its time format, width and how long it stays up, and whether it stays up while a video plays.

The older settings for it are still in the settings reference. While viewer.overlay-layout is empty, they make the layout.

Crop, aspect ratio and the backdrop

A video often has black bars, or a blurred backdrop, round its picture. rsimgv can crop to the picture and fill the space round it with a blurred copy of itself.

Toggle Crop Detection
video.toggle-crop turns cropping on or off.
Crop method
video.crop.method chooses how the picture is found: mpv's near-black border detector (cropdetect), the brightness steps at the picture's border (edges), the area that moves between frames (static), or the border steps combined over several frames (across).
Crop shape
video.crop.ratio keeps the detected shape (auto) or narrows the crop to 4:3, 16:9, 9:16, 1:1, 3:2, 2:3 or 21:9. video.cycle-crop-ratio steps through them, and video.cycle-aspect cycles the output aspect ratio.
Trim
video.crop.trim cuts this many pixels from each side that was cropped, so a soft edge is not left in.
Edit Crop
video.edit-crop shows the video uncropped with a draggable rectangle over it. Drag its edges or corners, or type the left, top, right and bottom edge in pixels; Apply keeps it, Cancel leaves the crop as it was.
Lock Crop
video.lock-crop holds the crop the video has now so it stops being detected, or lets it be found again.
Output aspect ratio
video.set-aspect sets the shape the picture is shown in, from Original or 4:3, 16:9, 9:16, 1:1, 3:2, 2:3 or 21:9. It is applied after the crop, and can be remembered per video with remember.aspect.
Set Thumbnail Frame
video.set-thumbnail-frame makes the frame the video shows now the one its thumbnail is made from. Video thumbnails can follow the locked crop (thumbnails.crop-video).

The crop and backdrop settings are on the Framing tab of Videos. The Backdrop group fills the space round the picture with a blurred, dimmed copy of it. Backdrop zoom, Backdrop blur and Backdrop brightness set how it looks, and video.toggle-backdrop turns it on or off.

Remembered position and speed

rsimgv can remember where you left a video, the speed you were playing it at, its volume and mute state, its output aspect ratio, and how it was turned or mirrored. Remembered → File settings chooses which. A video left within two seconds of the start or the end starts from the beginning instead. The memory is keyed by the file's contents rather than its name, so it survives moving or renaming; see per-file settings.

Settings that change as a video plays

A video's turn, mirror, zoom, crop and speed can be set to change at points in its timeline. Position the video, set it up as you want it from there on, and press K (video.timeline-set). Those settings then apply from that position, in place of any set for about the same place, and the ones before it hold until it.

  • J and L skip to the previous and next time the settings change (video.timeline-previous, video.timeline-next).
  • Shift+K removes the settings that apply at the position, so the earlier ones hold again (video.timeline-remove).
  • Ctrl+Shift+K removes every timed setting from the video (video.timeline-clear).

Zooming and panning a video, 0 to show all of it again, is done with an mpv crop filter, and is applied after the crop and the turn.

Previews in the browse grid

In the browse grid a video's thumbnail is a frame a tenth of the way in. While it is the active item, its preview can play a strip of frames spread through the video, made with ffmpeg, or an embedded player in the item's frame. Browsing → Items → Thumbnails → Video previews chooses which, or none.

Choosing the backend

Player backend on the Videos → Playback page chooses what plays videos. The change applies to the next video you open.

{{shot prefs-videos "The Videos page of the preferences"}}
mpv (default)
libmpv, rendered into memory for GTK to show, or with OpenGL when video.mpv.opengl is on. SVP, crop, the backdrop and mpv options apply to this backend.
gtk
GStreamer, through GTK's own media backend.

Configuring mpv

rsimgv starts mpv with sensible defaults: no window of its own, hardware decoding set to auto-safe, keep-open so a finished video stays on its last frame, no on-screen controller, and mpv's default key bindings off, because rsimgv handles the keys. Two places let you change that:

  1. mpv options in Videos → Playback take mpv command-line options such as --hwdec=auto-copy. Options that would give mpv a window of its own (vo, wid, gpu-context, fs, fullscreen) are ignored.
  2. mpv.conf in ~/.config/rsimgv/mpv/ is an ordinary mpv config file kept with rsimgv's settings, along with mpv's scripts. rsimgv watches it, and players made after a change use the new options.

Render videos with OpenGL (video.mpv.opengl) uses libmpv's OpenGL renderer instead of the software one, with the blurred backdrop drawn by an mpv shader.

Driving mpv directly

Each player listens for mpv's JSON IPC on /tmp/rsimgv-mpv-<pid in hex> unless your options name another socket, so tools that speak mpv's protocol can reach it. The socket appears only once a video has been opened.

Rotated videos

libmpv's software renderer cannot rotate frames, and a quarter turn makes it fail. rsimgv leaves the renderer's rotation off and turns such videos upright with a filter instead, so a phone video shot in portrait plays the right way up. Turning a video while watching it works the same way as turning a picture.
