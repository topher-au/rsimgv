title: Keybindings and mouse desc: How rsimgv's contextual keybindings work, every action with its default keys, the keybinding editor and mouse options. ---

Every key press is resolved against a table of bindings that depends on where you are. The same key can move the cursor in the browse view, pan a picture, and raise the volume of a video.

{{shot keybinds-overlay-viewer "F1 shows the keybindings in force where you are"}}

Contexts

A binding belongs to a context. There are seven, and a more specific context wins when two of them bind the same key:

ContextActiveSpecificity
GlobalEverywhere.Least specific
BrowsingWhile you browse a folder.More specific
ViewerIn the viewer, whatever it shows.More specific
PictureWhile the viewer shows a picture.Most specific
VideoWhile the viewer shows a video.Most specific
PlaylistWhile the playlist has the focus, on top of browsing or the viewer.Most specific
Folder TreeWhile the folder tree has the focus, on top of browsing or the viewer.Most specific

That is why ↑ pans a picture up and raises a video's volume: image.pan-up is bound in Picture and video.volume-up in Video. Picture and Video sit inside Viewer, so a viewer binding applies to both unless one of them binds the same key itself. The playlist and the folder tree have the focus while browsing or viewing, so their bindings sit on top of both.

An action has a home context, which its name's prefix gives (browse., view., image., video., playlist., tree., or win. and app. for Global). Actions that act on the window can be bound in any context. Actions that need what they act on, such as the browse cursor, a picture or a video, can be bound where that is, or in Global. Viewer actions can be bound in the viewer as a whole, or only for pictures or only for videos. Two settings that change what the viewer lists as well, hidden files and media-only, can be bound anywhere.

See what is bound: F1

Press F1 to show a list of the keybindings in force where you are: the ones for browsing, or for the viewer, a picture or a video, and the global ones. The list sits in a corner, lets clicks through, and moves away from the pointer when it gets near. Press F1 again to hide it.

The keybinding editor

Open it with Ctrl+Shift+,, or from Preferences → Controls → Keyboard.

{{shot prefs-keybindings "The Keyboard page of the preferences"}}
  • Every action is listed with its icon, description, context and keys.
  • The search box at the bottom filters by action name or shortcut.
  • Add a key with +, press the combination you want, and choose its context. If it conflicts with a binding that could also be pressed in an overlapping context, you are told.
  • Some actions take extra settings per binding: the seconds, frames, percentage, speed or angle of the video and rotation actions, the script to run, a notification's message, level and duration, or a target such as the video or the application for the volume actions, or an aspect ratio for video.set-aspect.
  • Undo steps back through your edits.

Only the chords that differ from the defaults are saved, to keybindings.yaml, so new default keys in later versions still reach you for the actions you have not touched.

The bindings file

The file sits beside the settings file. A plain chord is a string; a binding with a value, a target, a script, a notification or a non-default context is a mapping with a key and the extra fields.

schema: 1
actions:
  view.next:
    - period
    - key: space
      context: image
  video.seek:
    - key: shift+right
      value: 30
  video.volume-up:
    - key: ctrl+shift+up
      target: application
  video.set-aspect:
    - key: ctrl+9
      target: "16:9"
  script:
    - key: ctrl+shift+r
      script: Reveal in file manager
  win.notify:
    - key: ctrl+shift+n
      message: Hello from rsimgv
      level: success
      seconds: 3

Chords are written as modifiers joined with + to a key name, such as ctrl+shift+equal, alt+left, page_down, bracketright or f6. Modifiers are ctrl, alt, shift and super, in any order and case. The context of a binding is one of global, browse, view, image, video, playlist or tree; leave it out for the action's own. The editor writes this file for you.

Mouse and scroll wheel

The Mouse tab of Controls sets what the side buttons and the wheel do.

{{shot prefs-mouse "Mouse and scrolling preferences"}}
Back button, Forward button
One of back, up, previous, next, forward or none. By default back and forward go through the folder history.
Scrolling on images
ctrl-zoom (the default) zooms while Ctrl is held, zoom zooms always, step steps between items, none does nothing.
Scrolling on videos
volume, seek, step between items, or none (the default).
Scroll speed
How far each notch of the wheel moves, as a percentage of the normal amount, from 10 to 400.
